# Field methods, prompts and the visibility checklist

Use when planning fieldwork, writing an interview guide, or reading transcripts for tenure content.

## Methods that work, and what each yields

| Method | Yield |
|---|---|
| Key informant interviews: basin agency, public works, agriculture, industry, planning, water utilities, village heads, water user group leaders, village water officials | Formal allocation chain, gate operations, permit difficulties, official views of informal use |
| Village FGDs with social mapping, stakeholder mapping, photovoice on transect walks, priority ranking | Locations and counts of pumps, canal openings, wells; problems ranked by farmers |
| Women-only interviews and FGDs, at home, in the respondent's language | Household and hygiene water, costs, exclusion from water user groups, unpaid labour |
| Interviews with persons with disabilities | Physical access barriers, exclusion from meetings |
| Multi-stakeholder and validation FGDs | Official positions on toleration, enforcement, reforms; corrections to findings |
| Drone mapping, community mapping, GPS-tagged attribute collection | Georeferenced inventory of pump points, canal openings, wells, sacred wells per village |
| Agency geospatial datasets converted to tables | Structures, small reservoirs, wells with permit fields (often blank), hydrological stations |

Methods from other countries (FAO 2024, *Water Tenure Perspectives*, printed pages):

- **Warna, India** (Ch. 5, p. 56): transect walks, plus timelines of use and conflict, rights and
  obligations, and power.
- **Morogoro, Tanzania** (Ch. 9, pp. 125-127): a household survey with a stated confidence level,
  and sex-separated FGDs.
- **Spain** (Ch. 6, pp. 75-81): satellite and aerial mapping of irrigated area set against the
  register.
- **Berrechid, Morocco** (Ch. 8, p. 111): a field inventory of abstraction points against the
  agency count.
- **West Bank** (Ch. 7, p. 90): well-company accountant records as archives of hours used.
- **Rwanda, Senegal, Sri Lanka** (Ch. 15, pp. 205, 217): remote sensing (WaPOR) with validation
  roundtables.

FAO's assessment guidance also recommends: at least two rounds of fieldwork, the first ending in a
preliminary report; participatory GPS mapping of every source; georeferencing the point of diversion,
the area of use and the return flow; and a scoping meeting where stakeholder groups check the
typologies and may add local categories.

## Questions that surface tenure

Ask about practice, not law. Ask "what makes the permit hard to get?", not "why don't you have a
permit?".

**Any user**

- Where does your water come from, for each use (drinking, cooking, bathing, washing, ablution,
  fields, livestock, ponds)? And in the dry season? When the canal is closed for maintenance?
- Who decides when and how much water you get? How do you know when it is your turn?
- What do you pay, to whom, in money or in kind, per what unit of land or time?
- What work must you contribute (cleaning canals, guarding at night)?
- What happens if someone takes water out of turn? Has it happened? Who settled it?
- Who cannot get water here, or gets it last? Why?
- Do you think your access will continue? What could take it away?
- Has this changed? Since when? Why?

**Irrigation**

- Which canal, which gate, which block? Is your plot at the head or the tail? Is it reached by a
  tertiary canal at all?
- Is there a rotation? Is it written anywhere? Is it followed?
- Who opens the gate? Can a farmer open it? What does it take to get it opened?
- Do you pump? From where? Individual or group pump? Who owns it? How is the fee agreed?
- Do you rent out or rent in land? Who arranges water for the renter?
- Does any ceremony or custom decide when planting or irrigation starts?

**Households, women, persons with disabilities** (Indonesian wording, then English)

- *Untuk keperluan sehari-hari, air Ibu dari mana saja?* Where does your daily water come from: own
  well, a neighbour, a public washing place, bought, river, mosque?
- *Dalam sebulan, berapa biaya air, termasuk listrik untuk pompa?* What does water cost in a month,
  including electricity for the pump?
- *Ada giliran air? Menurut Ibu adil atau tidak? Ada blok yang didahulukan?* Is there a water
  rotation? Is it fair? Is any block favoured?
- *Kalau ada rapat soal air, siapa yang ikut memutuskan? Perempuan boleh ikut?* Who goes to water
  meetings and decides? May women attend?
- How long does getting water take each day? Who carries it? What happens when you are ill?

**Agencies, utilities, industry**

- What sources do you abstract from? Under what document? When does it expire? Is it metered?
- What uses in your area do you know of that have no permit? How do you deal with them?
- What priority do you get in a shortage? Who decides?

Record the question that produced each answer. A respondent's answer to "is this legal?" is data
about perceived security, and nothing more.

## Visibility checklist

Before closing the catalogue, ask of the evidence whether each of these is present, absent, or not
looked for. Write the answer in `report.md` section 3.

- [ ] Women's household, hygiene and menstrual-hygiene water, including in the fields
- [ ] Persons with disabilities: physical access, inclusion in decisions
- [ ] Landless labourers, sharecroppers, tenants, seasonal lessees (for example outside growers who rent land in the dry season)
- [ ] Non-resident or absentee landowners, and migrant-worker households
- [ ] Tail-end plots and upland plots no canal reaches
- [ ] Groundwater users: dug wells, driven wells, boreholes (often missing from basin water accounts)
- [ ] Purchased water: refill bottles, tankers, neighbours selling water
- [ ] Fishers, duck herders, pond operators
- [ ] Sacred and ritual sources, and ritual calendars that set irrigation timing
- [ ] Religious institutions as water sources (mosque and prayer-house water)
- [ ] Downstream users affected by upstream abstraction or pollution
- [ ] Environmental flows and wetlands
- [ ] Industry and mining abstracting without appearing in any register
- [ ] Older or historical arrangements that still shape access (colonial scheme design, former offices)
- [ ] Inheritance rules that pass water with land to sons only, and how widows and daughters get access
- [ ] Pastoralists and livestock watering points, rights of way
- [ ] People living off leaks, drains and return flows (gleaning, wastewater irrigation)
- [ ] The register-versus-field gap: count abstraction points on the ground and compare with the official list

## Local terms (Indonesia)

Spelling varies; record variants. Meanings vary by place: use the meaning given in your own cited
field sources, not this table. Where sources disagree, keep the disagreement. For other countries,
start an equivalent table in the project and cite each meaning to a source.

| Term | Common meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| bobokan | Opening in a canal wall, controlled with stones and sacks, feeding fields above or beside the tertiary | Definitions vary between sources; prefer the field description |
| oncoran | Unofficial offtake or leak along a canal | Also used for night-time diversion; check which |
| pompanisasi | Pumping from river, canal or drain, organised by a group or done individually | ASR transcripts may garble it |
| pipanisasi | Farmer-built piping from a canal to higher plots | |
| gilir giring | Rotation of water between blocks or villages in scarcity | Often not in any written policy |
| golongan | Fixed priority groups in an irrigation scheme (group I upstream first) | |
| blangko | Forms recording farmers' water needs for the annual plan | Who fills them in matters |
| raksabumi, ulu-ulu, jogotirto | Village water official; may also chair the water user group | Offices and pay differ by village and region |
| P3A, GP3A, IP3A, mitra cai | Water user groups at tertiary level, their federations; *mitra cai* in Sundanese areas | Pump groups and canal groups may be separate bodies |
| mantri air, PPA, juru, pengamat | Canal officer, gate operator, field officer, observer in the agency chain | |
| sedekah bumi, merti desa | Earth-thanksgiving ritual before planting; may set irrigation timing | |
| subak | Balinese irrigation society with its own customary rules and temple network | |
| bau/bahu, bata, tumbak | Land units (a bau is roughly 0.7 ha; a bata roughly 14 m2) | Convert only with a cited conversion |
| galon isi ulang | Refilled 19-litre drinking-water bottles | |
| embung, balong, kubangan | Small reservoir, pond, open pit | |
| SIPA, izin/persetujuan penggunaan sumber daya air | Water abstraction permits and approvals | Name them as respondents do; the legal stage interprets them |
| handil, tatas, beje, saka, tabat, baruh, pukung pahewan | Dayak Ngaju customary canals, channels, fish pits, creeks, weirs, ponds and protected areas, Kahayan peatlands | AlAfghani, Lee and Yasuda 2025, Table 2, citing Lubis (2013) and Satia et al. (2019) |
