# Chapter 7. Informal Tenure

Informal tenure covers uses that persist without a formal basis. It is the largest type in the
example catalogue, with five records, all on the Wening scheme and the river below it: canal-wall
openings (A003), collective river pumping (A004), dry-season pumping by land lessees (A005), hoses
from the canal (A017) and night-time gate opening (N01). The agency's field officer knows of the
first two and says he leaves them alone.

## 7.1 Definition and legal anchor

Informal tenure describes the use of water without a recognised formal basis, which persists and may
be tolerated by the authorities.^[{{FAO-LWDP10}}; {{FAO-UNPACK}}.] FAO's case studies separate
informal access from informal use and treat informality as a warning signal rather than a
verdict.^[{{FAO-PERSP}} 82-83.] This chapter records how officials are reported to treat each use;
whether any of them requires authorisation is referred (Section 7.8).

## 7.3 Where it occurs

All five records sit on the West secondary canal or the river mainstem below the Wening weir, in
Sukarukun Block 3 and in Karangwangi.^[{{S11}} rows 4-6; {{S04}} 3-4; {{NK01}} para 3.] The
openings lie between gates BW-3 and BW-4; the night-time gate opening is at BW-4.^[{{S11}} rows 4-6;
{{NK01}} para 3.]

## 7.4 How the arrangements work

**Canal-wall openings, Sukarukun Block 3 (A003).** Farmers whose plots sit above the tertiary canal
take water through three openings in the secondary canal wall, closed with sacks and stones when not
in use.^[{{S11}} rows 4-6; {{INT-V1-01}} l 41.] The agency's field officer said: '*Secara teknis
tidak boleh, tapi kami biarkan selama tidak ada yang mengeluh*'.^[{{INT-AG-01}} l 48: 'Technically
it is not allowed, but we leave it as long as nobody complains' (translation by the authors).]

**Collective river pumping, Karangwangi Block 3 (A004).** An unregistered pump group runs diesel pumps
in two shifts for rice and chilli farmers of an upland block the tertiary canal does not
reach.^[{{INT-V2-03}} l 41-55.] Members agree the fee by deliberation each season and pay 50 kg of
unhusked rice per 0.1 ha per season, halved on crop failure; a member who does not pay loses the next
turn.^[{{INT-V2-03}} l 52, 54.] The pump count differs between sources: 6 pumps according to a
member, 7 according to the team's mapping walk.^[{{INT-V2-03}} l 46; {{S04}} 3.] The group says it
would apply for a permit if it could, but has no legal status.^[{{INT-V2-03}} l 48: '*Kalau ada
izinnya kami mau urus, tapi kelompok kami belum berbadan hukum*', 'If there were a permit we would
apply, but our group has no legal status yet' (translation by the authors).]

**Dry-season lessees, Karangwangi (A005).** In the dry season landowners lease plots to onion growers
from outside the district, who bring their own pumps and run them day and night.^[{{INT-V2-03}} l
60-63; {{S06}} para 4.] The lease is paid to the landowner; no payment for water was reported. Local
farmers say water now follows ability to pay.^[{{INT-V2-03}} l 60-63.]

**Hoses from the canal, Dusun Krajan, Karangwangi (A017).** Five households run hoses from the
secondary canal for bathing and washing.^[{{S04}} 4.] When the canal is closed for maintenance,
women carry water from the river.^[{{INT-V2-05}} l 30-34.]

**Night-time gate opening, gate BW-4 (N01).** In the 2025 dry season, the gate at BW-4 was opened at
night outside the published schedule, and the neighbouring village complained to the
agency.^[{{NK01}} para 3-6.] The agency's institutional account posted the schedule again that week,
which corroborates the press report on place and time.^[{{NK01}} para 6; {{NX01}} para 1.]

**What they share.** Each use fills a gap the formal schedule leaves: plots above the canal, an
upland block below the weir, the dry season, and the hours between turns. None reported a sanction
beyond complaint, and two are known to the agency's officer and left alone.^[{{INT-AG-01}} l 48,
77.]

## 7.5 Who holds it, and who is left out

The users are smallholders on upland or tail-end plots, outside lessees, and households beside the
canal. Women bear the cost when the canal closes.^[{{INT-V2-05}} l 33.] Local farmers lose canal
level in the dry season to the lessees' pumps.^[{{S06}} para 5.]

## 7.6 Interaction with other tenure types

The pump group is informal with impossible tenure layered on it: it has no legal personality and so,
by its own account, could not hold a permit (A004). The lessees' pumping is a land-water nexus: a
land lease moves water use to people who hold no water arrangement (A005). Gate opening at night cuts
across agency control and commonhold on the same canal (A001, A002, N01).

## 7.7 Contested classifications and gaps

The pump counts are kept side by side, not averaged. The lessees were not interviewed, and
dry-season pumps were not counted; both are next steps in Annex K.
