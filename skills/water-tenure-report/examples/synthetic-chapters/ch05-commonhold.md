# Chapter 5. Commonhold

Water is held in common by a defined group of users. One record shows commonhold as its primary
type, the block rotation of the Sukarukun water user group (A002); two more carry it as a secondary
reading (A001, A008), and in one village the existence of the group is itself disputed (A016).

## 5.1 Definition and legal anchor

Commonhold describes rights to water held in common by a defined community of users, such as the
members of a water user organisation.^[{{FAO-LWDP10}}; {{FAO-UNPACK}}.] In this basin the agency
officer says the Sukarukun group is registered with the district.^[{{INT-AG-01}} l 31.] What that
registration confers is not decided here.

## 5.3 Where it occurs

The rotation runs on tertiaries T1 to T3 of the West secondary in Sukarukun, Blocks 1 to 3.^[{{INT-V1-01}}
l 30; {{S05}} slide 6.]

## 5.4 How the arrangement works

**Tertiaries T1-T3, Sukarukun (A002).** Within the village's weekly turn at the agency's gate, the
group rotates water between Blocks 1, 2 and 3, one day each, and repeats.^[{{INT-V1-01}} l 30-34;
{{INT-V1-02}} l 18.] The order is decided in the members' meeting: '*Giliran per blok, satu hari
satu blok, diputuskan di rapat anggota*'.^[{{INT-V1-01}} l 30: 'One day per block in turn, decided
in the members' meeting' (translation by the authors).] Members pay Rp 25,000 per 0.1 ha per season
and clean the tertiary canals twice a season.^[{{INT-V1-01}} l 33-34.] A member who opens a block
gate out of turn is fined one day's labour.^[{{INT-V1-01}} l 36.]

## 5.5 Who holds it, and who is left out

The group's members are rice farmers with land in the three blocks.^[{{INT-V1-01}} l 30.] None of
the 4 women interviewed in Sukarukun attends the members' meeting, so the women who manage household
water (Chapter 4) have no voice in the rotation.^[{{INT-V1-02}} l 22.] Members describe the rotation
as fair in the wet season; the record holds no account for the dry season.^[{{INT-V1-02}} l 20.]

## 5.6 Interaction with other tenure types

Commonhold depends on agency control: the group can only rotate the hours the agency's gate keeper
releases (A001). In Karangwangi, the village water official who sets the planting ritual also
chairs the water user group, so customary and commonhold authority sit with one person (A008,
Chapter 6).

## 5.7 Contested classifications and gaps

Whether a water user group operates in Karangwangi is contested. A machine-written village summary
says there is none; an interview and the team's findings deck say there is.^[{{S08}}
water_user_group.exists; {{INT-V2-03}} l 80; {{S05}} slide 9.] The machine summary is a derived
digest and cannot settle the question, so the record stays unclassified (A016, Annex J). The
district's registration list would decide it.
