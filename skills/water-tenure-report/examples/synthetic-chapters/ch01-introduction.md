# Chapter 1. Introduction

This report reviews and maps the water tenure arrangements of the Kali Wening basin, a fictional
river basin used to demonstrate the method. It is organised by tenure type: one chapter, or one
section of a chapter, for each type the evidence shows.

## 1.1 Purpose

Water tenure is the relationship, legally or customarily defined, between people, as individuals or
groups, with respect to water.^[{{FAO-LWDP10}}.] A tenure arrangement says who may take water,
from where, when, how much, on what conditions, and who decides. The purpose of this report is to
describe those arrangements as they work on the ground, with a source for every statement, and to
pass the legal questions they raise to a separate legal assessment.

## 1.2 The basin

The Wening scheme draws from the Kali Wening at a weir and serves rice fields through a West
secondary canal. The basin agency allocates water to the canal by priority group and sets a weekly
gate schedule.^[{{S01}} 12.] Below the weir, farmers of Karangwangi pump from the river
mainstem.^[{{S04}} 3; {{INT-V2-03}} l 41.] Upstream, households of the upland village of Tegalsari
take water from the Tuk Sepi spring.^[{{INT-V3-02}} l 12.] The district town of Mekarsari takes piped water from an
intake above the weir.^[{{S01}} 30.]

## 1.3 The FAO typology

FAO's water tenure work groups arrangements into two families: types derived from formal law, and
types not established on formal law.^[{{FAO-LWDP10}}; {{FAO-UNPACK}}.] The typology has been applied to
Indonesian basins, where most arrangements found were de minimis or customary and modern permits
attached mainly to estates, commerce and hydropower.^[{{JENRL}}; {{IOP2023}}.] This report uses the
typology as a classification grid, not as a statement of what the law of any country requires.

## 1.4 How to read this report

Part I covers rights granted or held by the state, Part II collective and community tenure, and
Part III tenure outside or against the state. Chapter 9 draws the patterns together. Record
identifiers in parentheses, such as (A004), point to the full record in Annex A. Footnotes cite the
sources; Annex E lists every source with its evidence grade. Questions for legal assessment are
listed at the end of each chapter and collected in Annex D.
