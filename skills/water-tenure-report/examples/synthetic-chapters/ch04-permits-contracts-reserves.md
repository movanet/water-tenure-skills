# Chapter 4. Permits, Supply Contracts, Reserves and De Minimis Use

Four thin types are kept in one chapter in this example, each in its own section; a real report
gives each type its own chapter and keeps every section of the template, however short. One record
each shows a modern permit (A012), a water supply contract (A011), a reserve (A018) and de minimis
use (A006).

## 4.1 Modern permits

**Starch factory, Sukarukun reach (A012).** The permit register records a permit for 25 l/s for 15
years, metered, held by a cassava starch factory.^[{{S02}} row 17.] Field use was not observed, so
the record is single-source and says nothing about how much the factory takes in the dry season.

## 4.2 Water supply contracts

**Utility intake above the weir, Mekarsari town (A011).** The allocation plan lists 120 l/s for the
district utility under a written bulk supply agreement.^[{{S01}} 30; {{INT-UT-01}} l 41.] The
utility's manager called renewal '*lama dan tidak pasti*'.^[{{INT-UT-01}} l 44: 'slow and
uncertain' (translation by the authors).] He also said the utility may not take water from
irrigation canals.^[{{INT-UT-01}} l 40-47.] Whether a rule says so is referred to legal assessment.

## 4.3 Reserves and minimum flows

**Below the Wening weir, Karangwangi (A018).** The allocation plan sets a minimum release of 300 l/s
below the weir.^[{{S01}} 40.] The agency's field officer says the release is not made in the driest
months, because irrigation demand takes priority.^[{{INT-AG-01}} l 90-92.] The record keeps both
versions and is graded contested. The reach below the weir is where about 30 households fish
(Chapter 8), so the gap between plan and practice falls on them.^[{{S10}} 7.]

## 4.4 De minimis rights

**Household boreholes, Sukarukun (A006).** Households pump shallow groundwater with small electric
pumps for drinking, cooking, bathing and washing.^[{{INT-V1-02}} l 40-44.] The team's drone layer
maps 4 boreholes.^[{{S11}} rows 9-12.] Women manage the supply in all 4 households interviewed and
pay the electricity, about Rp 40,000 a month.^[{{INT-V1-02}} l 41, 43.] Users call the wells secure
'because the well is on our land'.^[{{INT-V1-02}} l 45: '*Sumurnya di tanah sendiri, jadi aman*'
(translation by the authors).]
