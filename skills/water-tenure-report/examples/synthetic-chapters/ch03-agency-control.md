# Chapter 3. Agency Control

A public agency holds broad powers to allocate and operate water. In the Kali Wening basin one
record shows it: the basin agency allocates the West secondary canal's water by priority group and
locks the gates between turns (A001).

## 3.1 Definition and legal anchor

Agency control, sometimes called agency tenure, describes arrangements under which a public agency
such as an irrigation, river basin or water agency holds legal powers to abstract and allocate
water.^[{{FAO-LWDP10}}; {{FAO-UNPACK}}.] In this basin the record maps to the agency's annual
allocation plan.^[{{S01}} 12.] What powers the plan rests on is a question for legal assessment
(Section 3.8).

## 3.3 Where it occurs

The record sits at division structure BW-3 on the West secondary canal, serving three blocks of
Sukarukun at the head of the scheme.^[{{S01}} 12; {{INT-V1-01}} l 15.]

## 3.4 How the arrangement works

**West secondary, gate BW-3, Sukarukun (A001).** The allocation plan places Sukarukun in priority
group I. The agency's gate keeper opens the division gate for 72 hours a week and locks it in
between.^[{{S01}} 12; {{INT-V1-01}} l 15.] The water user group chair described the turn as
'*kami dapat giliran tiga hari seminggu*'.^[{{INT-V1-01}} l 15: 'we get a turn of three days a
week' (translation by the authors).] The agency describes the schedule as its own
allocation.^[{{INT-AG-01}} l 22.] No sanction beyond the locked gate was reported. Inside the
village, distribution between blocks passes to the water user group (Chapter 5).

## 3.5 Who holds it, and who is left out

The agency holds the gate; the users are rice farmers of the served blocks.^[{{S01}} 12.] The chair
calls the schedule reliable in the wet season and uncertain in the dry season.^[{{INT-V1-01}} l
19.] The record names no user group outside the served blocks; the upland and tail-end users who
take water outside the schedule appear in Chapter 7.

## 3.6 Interaction with other tenure types

Agency control at the gate is layered over commonhold inside the village: the agency allocates to
the village and the water user group allocates between blocks (A001, A002). The same canal also
feeds openings, hoses and night-time gate opening outside the schedule (A003, A017, N01).

## 3.7 Contested classifications and gaps

The record is corroborated, not triangulated: the plan and the agency officer are one leg, because
an agency describing its own allocation counts once, and the chair is the second.^[{{SKILL}} 'The
order of work', step 7.] The catalogue holds no record of agency control over the river below the
weir apart from the minimum release (A018); whether other agency-run works exist was not looked for.
