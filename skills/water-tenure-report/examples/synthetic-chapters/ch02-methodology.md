# Chapter 2. Methodology

## 2.1 The method in brief

The report rests on a catalogue: one record per tenure arrangement, each with its sources and
pinpoints, built with the `water-tenure-analysis` skill.^[{{SKILL}}.] Two catalogues were merged. The
first is a field catalogue of 18 records from interviews, a focus group discussion, a workshop,
agency records, team mapping and published material. The second is a 30-day scan of news and social
media, with 4 records. Each chapter was drafted from an evidence pack holding every record of its
tenure type, and every statement cites its source by token.

## 2.2 Evidence grades

Each source carries an evidence grade that describes the kind of source, not its truth. The grades
are: E1 primary legal or official; E2 field primary; E3 scholarly or technical; E4 press; E5 social
media; D derived digest. A derived digest is a pointer to an original and never evidence on its own.

![Sources and records by evidence grade](figures/fig-grades.png)

Social media are clues. A statement resting only on E5 or D sources does not appear as a finding in
the body of a chapter; it goes to the chapter's Clues section and to Annex I with what would
corroborate it. Where a post agrees with E1 to E4 evidence on place, time and actor, the text may say
that it corroborates that evidence. Private accounts are not identified.

## 2.3 Confidence

Confidence is a property of the record, separate from the grade of its sources. A record is
triangulated when three or more independent origins across two or more primary source types support
it; corroborated with two independent origins; single-source with one first-hand source;
uncorroborated when it rests on hearsay or an allegation; and contested when sources disagree. Two
documents drawn from one round of fieldwork count as one origin.^[{{SKILL}} 'The order of work',
step 7.]

![Confidence of records, by tenure type](figures/fig-confidence.png)

## 2.4 Classification

Each record carries one primary tenure type and, where the evidence supports it, secondary types. A
record is classified on reported practice and on how officials are reported to treat it. Where the
evidence does not allow a call, the record stays unclassified and says what would decide it. Legal
premises are recorded as reported facts; the legal questions go to Chapter 9 and Annex D, and are
not answered here.^[{{SKILL}} 'Rules that are not negotiable'; {{FAO-PERSP}} 35-36.]

## 2.5 Merging the catalogues

Records were not deleted or fused. Where the media scan and the field catalogue describe the same
place and mechanism, both records are kept and cited together. The media scan's source identifiers
carry the prefix N so that the two registers cannot collide.

## 2.6 Spatial method

Each record is located administratively and hydrologically. The maps count records per village named
in the administrative location; only coordinates stated by a source are plotted, and none was
estimated.

![Records by map unit, with source-stated coordinates](figures/fig-records-map.png)

## 2.7 The AI-assisted pipeline and the human role

In a real assessment this section names who set the questions, the scope and the method, which
steps AI agents carried out, and where a person reviewed the work. For this example, the catalogues
were written by hand and the chapters drafted by an AI agent; no person has read the records against
sources, because the sources do not exist.
