# About This Report {.unnumbered}

This is a worked example of the `water-tenure-report` skill. Every place, person, organisation,
source, quotation and number in it is invented. The Kali Wening basin, Arunika Province and the
villages named here do not exist, and the map layers were drawn for the example.

**How this example was made.** The two input catalogues were written by hand for the skill
repository. The scripts of the skill merged them, graded the sources, drew the figures, generated
the annexes and resolved the citation tokens into footnotes. The chapter text was drafted by an AI
coding agent following the skill's drafting brief, as a demonstration of the chapter template.

**In a real report**, this page states who directed the work and set the method, which AI agents and
models drafted which parts, what a person reviewed, and what no person has yet read against its
sources. Annex H gives the detail.

# Executive Summary {.unnumbered}

The merged catalogue holds 22 arrangement records drawn from 27 sources: 18 records from a field
catalogue and 4 from a 30-day media scan. They fall into 11 of the FAO tenure types, and 2 records
remain unclassified (Annex J). One further type, impossible tenure, appears only as a secondary
reading of a pump group that has no legal personality (A004).

**Part I, rights granted or held by the state.** The basin agency controls the gates of the Wening
scheme and allocates water by priority group and by hours (A001). Written arrangements exist for a
utility's bulk supply (A011) and a factory's permit (A012). The allocation plan sets a minimum
release below the weir, which the agency's field officer says is not made in the driest months
(A018).

**Part II, collective and community tenure.** A registered water user group rotates water between
blocks inside one village (A002). Wells, a spring rotation and a planting ritual are governed by
custom (A007, A008, A009), and a mosque well is open to anyone (A015).

**Part III, tenure outside the state.** Canal-wall openings, collective river pumping, dry-season
pumping by land lessees, hoses from the canal and night-time gate opening persist without a permit
and are known to the agency (A003, A004, A005, A017, N01). A village pipeline is treated as official
without a register entry (A010). Fishers, duck herders and sand operators use the river under no
water arrangement at all (A013, A014, N03).

**What the catalogue cannot settle.** Whether one village has a water user group (A016), where
tanker water comes from (N02), and every question in Annex D, which is for legal assessment.
