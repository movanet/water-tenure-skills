# Chapter 6. Customary and Religious-Law Tenure

Three arrangements draw their authority from custom: two sacred wells and their ritual (A007), a
planting ritual that times irrigation (A008), and a spring rotation said to date from the founders of
a hamlet (A009). A mosque well is shared on religious terms (A015). A social-media video of a spring
ritual is a clue only (N04).

## 6.1 Definition and legal anchor

Customary tenure covers arrangements whose rules claim authority from custom, ancestors, ritual or a
customary office; religious-law tenure covers water held or shared under religious rules or
institutions.^[{{FAO-LWDP10}}; {{FAO-UNPACK}}.] The records name no village or district instrument
that recognises these arrangements; whether one exists is referred (Section 6.8).

## 6.3 Where it occurs

Two sites are in Karangwangi, Dusun Krajan: the sacred wells and the mosque well.^[{{S04}} 5;
{{FGD-V2-01}} l 90.] The spring rotation is at the Tuk Sepi spring in Tegalsari, in the upper
catchment.^[{{INT-V3-02}} l 12.]

## 6.4 How the arrangements work

**Sacred wells, Dusun Krajan, Karangwangi (A007).** About 120 households draw from two wells in the
dry season.^[{{FGD-V2-01}} l 56; {{S04}} 5.] They use their own buckets and small pumps.^[{{INT-V2-05}} l 12-20.] An annual
well-cleaning ritual led by elders opens dry-season use; access rules beyond the ritual were not
described.^[{{INT-V2-05}} l 12-20; {{FGD-V2-01}} l 55-60.] The village government contributes to the ritual.^[{{FGD-V2-01}}
l 58.]

**Planting ritual, Karangwangi (A008).** The village water official announces the date of the
*sedekah bumi* (earth ritual), and canal water is not let into the fields before it.^[{{INT-V2-03}}
l 70-74; {{FGD-V2-01}} l 80-82.] Because he also chairs the water user group, the ritual calendar and
the scheme schedule are set by the same person.^[{{INT-V2-03}} l 70-74.]

**Spring rotation, Tuk Sepi, Tegalsari (A009).** Water runs through a bamboo and pipe channel; each
household takes it for a fixed number of hours in rotation, and gardens only after houses.^[{{INT-V3-02}}
l 12-25; {{S07}} 45.] Households that miss the annual channel cleaning lose their next
turn.^[{{INT-V3-02}} l 24.] The elder said the rotation '*dari pendiri dusun, tidak pernah
berubah*'.^[{{INT-V3-02}} l 13: 'comes from the founders of the hamlet and has never changed'
(translation by the authors).]

**Mosque well, Dusun Krajan, Karangwangi (A015).** Anyone may draw from the mosque well; the caretaker
asks only that users keep the area clean.^[{{FGD-V2-01}} l 90-94; {{INT-V2-05}} l 40-42.]

## 6.5 Who holds it, and who is left out

Elders hold the wells' ritual and the spring rotation; the users are households. Water collection
falls to women: all 9 participants of the women's focus group collect water from the sacred
wells.^[{{FGD-V2-01}} l 57.] Women queue at the mosque well in the early morning in the dry
season.^[{{FGD-V2-01}} l 90-94.]

## 6.6 Interaction with other tenure types

Household pumping at the sacred wells is also a de minimis use (A007, secondary type). The planting
ritual overlaps commonhold (A008, Chapter 5). At Tuk Sepi, the elder fears the village pipeline
below the spring will take its flow (A009, A010).^[{{INT-V3-02}} l 30.]

## 6.7 Contested classifications and gaps

The access rules of the sacred wells outside the ritual period are unknown and should be asked of
the elders. The spring rotation rests on one interview and one published article; a second field
source would raise its confidence.
