# Chapter 8. Assumed, Unrecognised and Impossible Tenure

This chapter groups three thin types and the records that could not be classified. A village pipeline
is treated as official with no permit on the register (assumed, A010). Fishers, duck herders and sand
operators use the river under no water arrangement (unrecognised, A013, A014, N03). Impossible tenure
has no primary record and appears only as a secondary reading of the pump group in Chapter 7 (A004).

## 8.1 Assumed water rights

Assumed tenure describes public or quasi-public uses that users and officials treat as lawful though
no instrument is in evidence.^[{{FAO-LWDP10}}; {{FAO-UNPACK}}.]

**Village pipeline below Tuk Sepi, Tegalsari (A010).** The village government built an intake below
the spring and a pipeline to two lower hamlets, charging Rp 15,000 per household per
month.^[{{INT-V3-02}} l 33-35.] The register of abstraction permits has no entry for
it.^[{{S02}} rows 1-48.] The elder explained: '*Itu proyek desa, jadi semua anggap
resmi*'.^[{{INT-V3-02}} l 33: 'It is a village project, so everyone considers it official'
(translation by the authors).] The absence of a register entry is reported as a fact; whether it is
conclusive is referred.

## 8.2 Unrecognised tenure

Unrecognised tenure covers livelihoods that use water but that water law does not address as water
use.^[{{FAO-LWDP10}}; {{FAO-UNPACK}}.]

**River fishers below the weir, Karangwangi (A014).** About 30 landless households fish the reach
below the weir with nets and traps.^[{{S10}} 7-9.] Catches fall when dry-season pumping lowers the
river.^[{{S10}} 7-9; {{S06}} para 8.] Fishers say nobody asks them when water is
allocated.^[{{S10}} 9.]

**Sand extraction, Karangwangi reach (N03).** In September 2025 farmers blocked sand trucks on the
river road, saying extraction lowers the river at their pump intakes.^[{{NK03}} para 2-5; {{NV01}}
00:01:20.] The operators' authorisation was not reported.

**Duck herders on the canals, Sukarukun (A013).** After the first-season harvest, herders from outside
the district bring flocks along the canals for several weeks.^[{{INT-V1-02}} l 60-61.] A woman farmer
said that locals assume the herders pay someone to be allowed in, and that she did not know
whom.^[{{INT-V1-02}} l 62.] The claim is hearsay and is kept as uncorroborated; the map export leaves
it out.

## 8.3 Impossible tenure

Impossible tenure describes holders who cannot hold a right because they lack legal
capacity.^[{{FAO-LWDP10}}; {{FAO-UNPACK}}.] No record carries it as its primary type. The pump group of
Chapter 7 carries it as a secondary reading because, by its own account, it has no legal status with
which to apply for a permit (A004).^[{{INT-V2-03}} l 48.] Whether other unregistered collectives
exist in the basin was not looked for, so the type is under-searched rather than shown to be absent.

## 8.4 Unclassified records

Two records could not be classified (Annex J). Whether Karangwangi has a water user group is
disputed (A016, Chapter 5). Households of upper Tegalsari buy tanker water in the dry season at a
reported Rp 150,000 per 5,000-litre tank; the price figure is UNVERIFIED because the article body
loads by script, and where the sellers take the water is not reported (N02).^[{{NK02}} para 4.]
